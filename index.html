About this map

A siting screen for LIHTC applications in Connecticut, Rhode Island and Massachusetts. Each state's Qualified Allocation Plan scores geography differently and the three scales are not comparable — only the state you select is coloured, and its legend states the denominator.

Built 4 August 2026. Items marked live are fetched from the publisher every time the page opens and stay current on their own. Items marked frozen were transcribed from a document on the build date and will not update themselves.

How far to trust the utility layers

All three states built these the same two ways: where a utility supplied pipe locations, a 300-foot buffer was drawn around the lines; where a town is 97%+ served and nothing better existed, the municipal boundary was used as the service area. A polygon edge is therefore often a cartographic artifact rather than a pipe. MassDEP states plainly that its data "is not appropriate for site-specific or parcel-specific analysis"; the same caveat applies to CT and RI, less loudly. Connecticut's water ESA layer is the weakest — 2017–18 vintage, and it maps who is entitled to serve, not where mains exist. Confirm service with the municipality before relying on any of it. This matters most for Rhode Island's conditional 5-point tier, the one place a service-area boundary decides a score.

Not mapped, and why

MA — MCAS school quality (8 pts)—The QAP scores "Advanced or Proficient" 10th-grade MCAS, a scale DESE no longer reports. The reference chart linked from EOHLC's own page is from 2013. Unresolved — a question for EOHLC.
MA — site-level B-6 components—Transit within 0.5 mi (4 pts), higher education within 2 mi (2), health care within 1 mi (2)
MA — Housing Production Plan communities (2 pts, B-2)— Deliberately excluded. EOHLC publishes status only through an interactive dashboard, not a machine-readable list, and the page was last updated September 2024. More importantly, HPP status is volatile — plans run five years and certification is re-earned annually — so a frozen list would go stale quickly and quietly. Check the EOHLC status table directly.
MA — Priority Development Areas (2 pts, B-2)—Designated by regional planning agencies; coverage uneven, no single statewide service
MA — formal neighborhood plans (2 pts, B-2)—No statewide dataset exists; municipality by municipality
CT — Location Index—Scored by census tract, not town; drives the Location Set-Aside
RI — Community criterion B—QCT / CCRP (1 pt) and graduation rates (2 pts)

If you are re-using this later

Re-check, in this order: (1) whether CHFA adopted the 2027–2028 guideline and how it changed; (2) the current RI QAP — as of 1 January 2026 the Executive Office of Housing writes it, not RIHousing, and a 2027 draft was in public comment in August 2026; (3) the current EOHLC QAP and a newer SHI, which is updated roughly every two years; (4) RIHousing's newer LMIH chart, published annually. The live layers above need no action.

Only the active state is colored. The other renders neutral so you keep the geography without implying the two scales mean the same thing — they don't.
